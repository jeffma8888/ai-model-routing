# Judge benchmarks (public evidence, as of 2026-10-29)

Cold-start priors only. These numbers are not agreement rates on this library's tasks and must not clear a routing bar (DESIGN.md section 4.4).

Model ids below use the provider-neutral form `provider/name`. Where a paper used a product name (Codex, Claude Code) the setting column says so.

## Summary

- No public RewardBench 2, JudgeBench, or 2026 human-agreement leaderboard row was found for anthropic/claude-opus-5.5, anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5, anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5, anthropic/claude-fable-5.1, anthropic/claude-fable-5, openai/gpt-6.1-sol, openai/gpt-6-sol, openai/gpt-6-astra, openai/gpt-6-luna, openai/gpt-5.6-terra, openai/gpt-5.6-luna, xai/grok-4.7, or xai/grok-4.
- The only models of interest with a published judge measurement are openai/gpt-5.6-sol and anthropic/claude-opus-5, and only as agentic proof checkers, not as scored graders on a shared human-labeled set (Shabadi et al., arXiv:2609.33773, 2026-09-27, paper).
- On held-out proofs, learned mutation strategies made planted logical errors 1.5x more likely to evade a Codex + openai/gpt-5.6-sol judge and 3.5x more likely to evade a Claude Code + anthropic/claude-opus-5 judge, both at medium reasoning (same paper).
- Raising reasoning effort did not close that gap on a 12-mutation rejudge: openai/gpt-5.6-sol at ultra still missed roughly half, and anthropic/claude-opus-5 at max missed all of them (same paper).
- RewardBench 2 is a best-of-4 accuracy benchmark (one chosen, three rejected; Ties uses a weighted margin), not a human pairwise-agreement rate. Its public results snapshot (dataset last modified 2025-12-11) stops at anthropic/claude-opus-4 (20250514) at 76.5 average and openai/gpt-4.1; no model of interest is in the file list (paper Table 3, HTML of arXiv:2506.01937v2, updated 2026-04-23).
- JudgeBench (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.12784v2, updated 2025-04-05) is pairwise, objective-correctness, no reference answer in the vanilla prompt, and a self-generated reference in the Arena-Hard prompt. Its newest public rerun found here uses anthropic/claude-3.7-sonnet, not a model of interest (arXiv:2605.13695, 2026-05-13).
- Rubric self-preference survives objective checkers: on LiveCodeBench, openai/gpt-5 (not a model of interest) was up to about 20x more likely to mark its own failed rubrics as passed than unrelated models' failures (Pombal et al., arXiv:2604.06996v3, updated 2026-08-03).
- On HealthBench's subjective rubrics, the same paper reports openai/gpt-5 giving itself about a 4 point bonus on a 100-point scale versus its own average bias, and says self-preference can skew model scores by up to 10 points.
- Blind authorship removes the usual self-preference effect, but the words "self" and "other" alone still move scores in opposite directions (Chae et al., arXiv:2608.18091v3, updated 2026-10-16).
- Label-free rubric edits that raise inter-judge agreement do not raise agreement with humans: on SummEval, Krippendorff's alpha rose by 0.069 while Kendall tau with experts changed by -0.007, CI crossing zero (Han and Kim, arXiv:2610.13303, 2026-10-09).

## Judge bias evidence

Settings matter more than the headline "judges like themselves."

- Same-model authorship, agentic proof checking. Shabadi et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33773, 2026-09-27) use the judge model as its own mutator. Held-out, strategy-guided logical edits evade openai/gpt-5.6-sol 1.5x more often and anthropic/claude-opus-5 3.5x more often than unguided edits. This is a self-audit of miss rate on adversarial errors, not a same-family preference rate against a second family's prose. Higher effort did not fix the selected misses (ultra still about half; max still all). Cross-check at medium effort: openai/gpt-5.6-sol misses Claude-written mutations more often than anthropic/claude-opus-5 misses GPT-written ones, on every dataset. The paper says Claude spends longer on long proofs (Appendix E); that is their explanation, not an independent replication.
- Rubric grading, objective gold. Pombal, Rei, and Martins (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06996, v3 2026-08-03) define HSPP-R as the judge's false-pass rate on its own failed rubrics divided by its false-pass rate on other families. Above 1 means self-preference after leniency is divided out. It stays above 1 on IFEval and LiveCodeBench, where a program is the gold label, so the bias is not just taste. Pairwise comparison was worse than single-rubric or direct assessment. A five-family majority vote lowered the ratio and did not remove it. Negative rubrics and subjective themes (communication, emergency referral) amplified it on HealthBench; accuracy-themed rubrics less so. Models in that study are one generation behind the models of interest (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Haiku 4.5).
- Family preference vs self preference. The same paper's HealthBench centered-delta matrix shows a diagonal (self) and a within-family block. Llama judges in that run did not show a strong self or family bonus. Do not copy the GPT-5 "+4 points" onto openai/gpt-5.6-sol or any GPT-6 model; it was not measured.
- Label-only bias, no stylistic fingerprint. Chae et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18091, v3 2026-10-16) judge sets of narrative constraints instead of generations. Blind, self-preference goes away. With quality matched, the bare labels "self" and "other" still raise and lower scores. A routing prompt that says "this output is from you" or names the generator family is therefore a different task from a blind judge call.
- Sanity check on older narcissism claims. Roytburg et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22548, v4 2026-06-22) retest published self-preference against an evaluator-quality null: only 51% of examples stay significant, but those hold 89.6% of the probability mass. Treat older "judges are narcissists" headlines as partly confounded, not as disproven.
- Format and position. Gondara et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17158, 2026-10-12): both comparative formats they tried had a slot bias whose sign flipped across judges. Binary verdicts tied the top score on 77-78% of prompts, so a pairwise judge with a tie option will often hand the decision to the tie-break rule. JudgeBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784) already double-runs every pair because order changes the verdict.
- Agreement with other judges is not agreement with humans. Han and Kim (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13303, 2026-10-09): editing the clauses judges disagree on raised Krippendorff's alpha by 0.069 on SummEval and did not move Kendall tau with experts (point estimate slightly down, CI includes zero). A panel that converges after rubric rewriting can be converging away from the gold the router is trying to match.

## Gaps

- No public number, for any model of interest, on human agreement for pairwise preference, rubric grading, faithfulness or hallucination detection, or code-correctness grading.
- RewardBench 2's Factuality subset is the closest public proxy to hallucination detection, and its Precise IF and Math subsets are the closest to instruction and code-like correctness, but the leaderboard does not include the models of interest and the metric is reward accuracy, not grader agreement.
- JudgeBench has a coding slice (42 of 350) but has not been rerun on these models in any paper opened here. The 2026-05-13 RTLC paper stops at Claude 3.7 Sonnet.
- No published same-family bias number for GPT-6 vs GPT-5.6, Claude 5.5 vs Claude 5, Fable vs other Claude models, or Grok judging Grok.
- The proof-fuzzing paper's per-dataset miss rates live in figures. This file records only the multipliers and the rejudge summary that are written in prose. Do not invent the bar heights.
- Pombal et al. Table 1 numeric cells did not survive HTML extraction as a clean table. The prose claims (about 20x, about 4 points, up to 10 points) are used; the full per-judge MRA table is not retyped.

## Caveats

- Public leaderboard accuracy is not the agreement rate this router measures. DESIGN.md 4.4: priors never clear a bar.
- RewardBench 2 scores are best-of-4 with three rejects, so they are not comparable to a pairwise preference accuracy and not comparable to JudgeBench. Random is well below 50%.
- Several RewardBench 2 subsets were themselves labeled by LLM judges (Factuality: two LLMs must agree; Safety: LM-as-a-judge plus rubrics). A high score can mean "agrees with the earlier judge," which is contamination of the gold, not human agreement.
- JudgeBench pairs come from one generator. That removes style confounds and adds a bias against that generator as a judge. The paper shows Claude 3.5 Sonnet falling from 64.3% on GPT-4o pairs to 44.8% on its own pairs.
- Shabadi et al. is a self-preference setup by construction: the same model writes the bug and grades the proof. Miss rates describe adversarial errors, which the authors say are not accuracy on natural mistakes. Two Claude Opus 5 (max) calls exited after 128k output tokens; those are failed calls, not verdicts.
- HealthBench "gold" in the rubric-bias paper is a majority of model judges, so the 4 point self bonus is disagreement with a panel, not with clinicians.
- Han and Kim's human-agreement null is on SummEval, HealthBench meta-evaluation, and USR Topical-Chat, with unnamed judges. It is a warning about rubric-optimization, not a score for any model of interest.
- Vendor self-report bias: none of the numbers in the Facts table are vendor benchmark blogs. The proof-fuzzing and rubric-bias papers still use vendor-hosted APIs and, for the agent paper, vendor orchestrators, so product-side fallback or hidden reasoning settings are not fully controlled. The RTLC and JudgeBench numbers are older than 120 days and are flagged in the table.
- Saturation: RewardBench 2 Focus and Safety are already in the mid-90s for strong reward models, so those slices will not separate frontier generative judges. Precise IF (top generative row in Table 3 is 61.9) and JudgeBench (best original general model 64%) are not saturated.
- This pass used arXiv HTML and the Hugging Face dataset API. The Gradio leaderboard at https://huggingface.co/spaces/allenai/reward-bench did not render rows (page shell only). Public web search returned no results for these queries in this session, so a leaderboard that exists only as a JS app may have been missed. Absence is "not found," not "does not exist."
