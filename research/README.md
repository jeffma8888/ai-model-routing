# Research behind the defaults

Public evidence gathered on 2026-10-27 to 2026-10-29, for datasets.md, hand-off-data.md and delegation-datasets.md on 2026-11-06, for the last sections of datasets.md and delegation-datasets.md on 2026-11-09, and for the context windows of delegation-datasets.md on 2026-11-10. Nearly every claim in these files cites a public URL and a date; the few textbook statistics references cited without a link are flagged as such in the Gaps section of prior-art.md. Vendor documentation rarely shows a publication date, so those entries record the date the page was opened.

| File | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| [judge-benchmarks.md](judge-benchmarks.md) | Is there public evidence on how well current models work as judges? (Short answer: almost none for the current generation, and what exists shows planted-error misses, self-preference and agreement that does not track human judgment) |
| [datasets.md](datasets.md) | Which public dataset could be the third source of committed evidence, with every model's per-item outcome on a coding or agentic task checked by a program? (Short answer: the tau2-bench paper results, MIT, with a per-item cost, but only four 2025 agent models. Its 2026-11-09 section asks for a dataset with many tasks whose best model differs and recommends LLMRouterBench, MIT since 2026-11-02, with a per-item cost and, on 4 of its 9 benchmarks scored by all 13 API models, another model ahead of the best single one by more than the calibration noise) |
| [hand-off-data.md](hand-off-data.md) | Is there public data that records a parent agent handing work to a sub-agent, with per-call token counts and a success label, to measure what the delegation evidence assumes? (Short answer: no; TRAIL records real hand-offs with per-call token counts, but no success label and no run of the same work inline) |
| [delegation-datasets.md](delegation-datasets.md) | Can the delegation evidence run on a second dataset, tau2-bench, or on a dataset with no cost? (Short answer: not without a harness change: each tau2-bench item is a conversation of 2 to 100 model calls, which the harness prices as one call or with one call count per grid point, and the OpenAI agents' recorded costs fit a prompt-cache discount, inferred from the costs; a `cost_blind`-only run would repeat the routing choice. Its 2026-11-09 section, with [llmrouterbench_prices.py](llmrouterbench_prices.py), asks the same of LLMRouterBench: not with the whole pool, since vendor pages price 4 of its 13 models and the recorded costs are one exact price pair for 5, which together price 8; its 2026-11-10 subsection gives each of those 8 a context window, 4 from vendor pages and 4 the open weights' position limit) |
| [no-path-clears.md](no-path-clears.md) | When no path clears the task's bar, should the delegation decision choose by calibration bound (`DESIGN.md` 13.5 row 7), by cost, or refuse? (Short answer: keep row 7, whose choice scores highest on the held-out halves of the committed delegation runs, and keep `refuse` as the default; on tau2-bench row 7 decides most `cost_effective` cells and mostly takes a path below the bar, which README Limitations says. Its numbers are the output of [no_path_clears.py](no_path_clears.py)) |
| [no-model-clears.md](no-model-clears.md) | When no model clears the task's bar, should a `cost_effective` router keep the model with the highest calibration bound (`DESIGN.md` 5.3) or take the cheapest? (Short answer: keep 5.3. On the four routing datasets that record a cost its fallback is the `best_on_calibration` model on every such cell; the cheapest model loses most of the held-out success on RouterBench and AutoBench, and saves cost for about the same success only where the models are close, as on tau2-bench and the AutoBench judges. Its numbers are the output of [no_model_clears.py](no_model_clears.py)) |
| [livecodebench-cost.md](livecodebench-cost.md) | Could the LiveCodeBench evidence carry a cost read from the length of its output text? (Short answer: no. On the OpenAI reasoning runs the visible output is about the same length at every reasoning setting, while their reasoning tokens are billed and not shown, so a text-length cost would price those settings alike; five vendors' current pages are read: the four that speak of billing say reasoning is billed as output, but the five name only `o4-mini` of the 67; their pricing pages list by their own name only the two Claude 4 models, with and without thinking, and `o3`; the pricing pages of `mistralai`, `moonshotai`, `qwen` and `meta-llama` list none of their runs, the four other open-weight families give no first-party price; the other eight families' pages are read too: three makers' API pages return the reasoning apart from the answer and name none of their runs, and of the five runs whose model cards describe a `<think>` block only one holds the marker in its first samples. So the note recommends keeping the evidence `cost_blind`: only those five runs carry a list price under their own name, and three of them were made with reasoning the files do not show in full. Its numbers are the output of [livecodebench_cost.py](livecodebench_cost.py)) |
| [where-routing-pays.md](where-routing-pays.md) | Where does task-level routing pay on the committed evidence, and what does a user's own data need for it to pay? (Short answer: on the first five routing files, in cost, at a bar below the best single model's held-out success, where cheaper models clear it, and not in success: three of them route one pooled task, on RouterBench's tasks one model takes most of the picks, and small calibration halves leave the Wilson bound well below the observed success. On LLMRouterBench, whose tasks differ in their best model, routing passes that success too, `cost_blind` at every bar at under half the best single model's cost. Its numbers are the output of [where_routing_pays.py](where_routing_pays.py)) |
| [routerbench-per-task.md](routerbench-per-task.md) | On RouterBench, how much success could per-task routing add if each eval took its own best model? (Short answer: almost none. One model is the best, or tied for the best, on every eval, and no other model leads any eval by more than the Wilson shortfall at its calibration half, so even chosen in hindsight each eval's own best model barely moves the held-out success, while the router, picking on calibration halves, ends below the best single model; the room is between prompts inside an eval. Its numbers are the output of [routerbench_per_task.py](routerbench_per_task.py)) |
| [llmrouterbench-seeds.md](llmrouterbench-seeds.md) | Does the LLMRouterBench gain over the best single model hold on each seed, or only in the mean over seeds that the committed results give? (Short answer: on each seed. `cost_blind` routing at every bar and `best_on_calibration` pass that model on every seed's own halves, while `cost_effective` routing passes it on every seed only at the highest bar and is below it on every seed at the low bars. Its numbers are the output of [llmrouterbench_seeds.py](llmrouterbench_seeds.py)) |
| [check-fallback.md](check-fallback.md) | On LLMRouterBench, what does the executor's move to the next model on a failed check buy, next to the per-item oracle? (Short answer: with a check as good as the record's score, an upper bound, two fallbacks recover about half to two thirds of the gap from the routed row to the oracle at two to three and a half times the cost, and every listed model nearly all of it, except where some model clears a high bar and the route holds only the models that clear it; only `arenahard`'s score needs no answer key and `livecodebench`'s public test cases give a partial check, so on the other seven tasks a caller has no such check at call time. Its numbers are the output of [check_fallback.py](check_fallback.py)) |
| [below-bar-fallback.md](below-bar-fallback.md) | On LLMRouterBench, should a route go on below the bar after its clearing models fail? (Short answer: as an option on `Task`, off by default. With a check as good as the record's score, an upper bound, two fallbacks rarely reach below the bar and add little, and every listed model reaches the per-item oracle for a little more cost, the gain sitting on the tasks where few models clear; but each answer it adds comes from a model under the bar that only the check vouches for, and on the tasks where it gains a caller has no check as good as the score. Its numbers are the output of [below_bar_fallback.py](below_bar_fallback.py)) |
| [where-delegation-pays.md](where-delegation-pays.md) | Does the per-task inline-or-delegate decision beat a fixed policy, always inline or always delegate, on the committed delegation evidence? (Short answer: rarely, and never on both success and cost. In `cost_effective` it equals always delegating on 403 of the 432 summary rows of the four delegation files, every row where inline work is a call of its own among them, and where the work folds into the parent's next call it trades success against cost on 26 of the 29 rows; in `cost_blind` a fixed policy has more held-out success at no more cost on 106 of the 432. Its numbers are the output of [where_delegation_pays.py](where_delegation_pays.py)) |
| [per-item-delegation.md](per-item-delegation.md) | On LLMRouterBench, how often is a held-out item's cheaper path, inline or delegated, by list price at its own sizes, not the per-task decision's, and what would taking it give? (Short answer: often, on 11% to 40% of the items in `cost_effective`, but taking it everywhere costs up to 7.8 points of mean success, and on the cells where the decision compared costs a rule that takes it saves at most 1.4% of a bar's summed cost, 8.1% on its best row. No spec row for now.) |

How the files feed the library:

- `model_routing/data/models.json`: vendor list prices and context windows, each row with its source URL, as of 2026-10-29
- `model_routing/data/priors.json`: three public leaderboards (Terminal-Bench 4.0, GDPval-AA, ARC-AGI-3), each with its source and date. Used only to order candidates at cold start, never to admit a model
- The default rules (generator isolation on, priors never admit, a conservative lower bound with a minimum sample count) follow from the findings in judge-benchmarks.md and prior-art.md
- `research/datasets.md` picked the third evidence dataset, the tau2-bench paper results, now committed under `evidence/tau2-bench/`
- `research/hand-off-data.md` is why the delegation evidence states its context and hand-off sizes as a grid and assumes a lossless brief
- `research/delegation-datasets.md` is why the delegation evidence read one dataset, the AutoBench matrix, until the tau2-bench conversations were priced on their own calls (`DESIGN.md` 13.12)
- `research/no-path-clears.md` is why `DESIGN.md` 13.5 row 7 stays as it is and `refuse` stays the default of `on_no_model`
- `research/no-model-clears.md` is why the `DESIGN.md` 5.3 `best_available` fallback stays the model with the highest calibration bound in `cost_effective` too
- `research/livecodebench-cost.md` measures what a cost read from output length would see on the LiveCodeBench files, and why it recommends keeping that evidence `cost_blind`
- `research/where-routing-pays.md` reads the committed routing results files and says where task-level routing pays on them and what a user's own data needs for it to pay
- `research/routerbench-per-task.md` reads the pinned RouterBench file, names each eval's best model and says what another dataset needs for per-task routing to gain in success
- `research/llmrouterbench-seeds.md` reruns the LLMRouterBench evaluation and says, seed by seed, whether routing's gain over the best single model holds on each split or only in the mean
- `research/check-fallback.md` replays the executor's fallback on a failed check over the LLMRouterBench records and says what it buys against the per-item oracle, and which task's score a caller could check at call time
- `research/below-bar-fallback.md` replays a route that goes on below the bar after its clearing models fail on the same records, and says why the library should offer it only as an option on `Task`, off by default
- `research/where-delegation-pays.md` reads the four committed delegation results files and says how often the per-task decision differs from always inline and from always delegating, and that where it differs it is never better than both
- `research/per-item-delegation.md` reruns the LLMRouterBench delegation evidence and says how often a held-out item's cheaper path differs from the per-task decision, and how little a rule that takes it saves where the decision compared costs

Caveats that apply to everything here:

- Leaderboards measure the models on someone else's tasks and harness; a gap of a point or two on one leaderboard is not a reliable ranking
- Headline numbers in papers are self-reported on the authors' benchmarks, usually with older models
- Prices and model versions change; treat every number as a dated snapshot
